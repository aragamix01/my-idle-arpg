# Roadmap

One rule decides this order: **anything that changes where damage comes from goes
before anything that authors numbers.** That is why Phase 5's formula rework jumped
the queue and shipped as 5a-1 and 5a-2, and it is why weapons-and-skills now sits in
front of the uniques - a skill supplies the base that every modifier multiplies, so
uniques authored first would be authored twice.

Order:  1 -> 5a-1 -> 5a-2 -> 6a -> 6b -> 2 -> 5a-3 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5b -> 5c

Status: [done] shipped and verified  [next] in progress or up next  [todo] not started

## [done] Phase 5a-1 - The three modifier layers
> Was Phase 5. Moved first: it replaces the damage formula, and Phase 2 and 4 both
> author numbers against that formula.

- Every modifier now declares a layer, and stats resolve as
      final = (base + sum flat) x (1 + sum increased) x product more
- flat and increased accumulate in a SUM, so their marginal value falls instead of
  rising. This is the fix for "it scales too fast": the old pool was all
  multipliers, so modifier N+1 was worth more than modifier N
- `more` is the only compounding layer. Reserved for uniques and the gold upgrade
  tracks, both bounded by something. A rollable `more` affix is rejected by
  validateRegistry
- Crit reworked: critChance = (base + flat) x (1 + increased), critMult additive
- Migration re-resolves every stored modifier value onto the new scale

Two things from the original plan turned out to be wrong and were dropped:
- Upgrade tracks CANNOT move to the `increased` layer. Enemy HP grows exponentially
  in stage; `increased` grows linearly in levels bought, and levels grow like
  log(gold). Linear against exponential walls permanently at any growth rate above
  1.0, and no retune fixes it. Tracks stay `more`
- Item-level-scaled flat tables are therefore unnecessary. A flat roll sits inside
  the multiplicative envelope, so every upgrade amplifies it and it never goes
  stale. No pegging, no ten-digit tooltips

Measured: drop ceiling 5.4x, craft 8.6x, offence/defence symmetry 0.008.
Stage 300 still reachable with no wall. Total elapsed shortened 4.7d -> 1.8d.


## [done] Phase 5a-2 - Modifier rows and the wider pool
- Rows per rarity: common 1 prefix 1 suffix, magic 2/2, rare 3/3
- The power ceiling deliberately did NOT move (5.4x drop, 8.6x craft). Per-affix
  values came down to pay for the rows. The gain is surface, not power: six
  modifiers to read instead of four, and a much wider gap between rarities
- Added a flat and an increased variant for each stat, since the two multiply each
  other - holding a lot of one makes the other the better pick
- Added defensive SUFFIXES. Widening the rows broke the offence/defence invariant
  structurally: offence could use 5 of its 6 rows, defence only 3, because every
  defensive affix was a prefix. Measured 0.47 asymmetry against a 0.15 bound
- Magic Ore and Dune Spirit descriptions corrected for the new row counts

Ladder after: stage 300 in 2.0d, clear times 18-29s, no wall.


## [next] Phase 6a - Weapons & skills, the foundation
> Was "Big change gameplay", at the bottom of the file. Moved to the front of the
> unbuilt work for the same reason Phase 5 was: it changes where damage COMES FROM,
> and Phase 2 authors eight uniques priced against that. Authoring them first means
> authoring them twice.

The good news, and the reason this is not another ground-up rework: **the three
layer system already accommodates skills.** Stats resolve as

      final = (base + sum flat) x (1 + sum increased) x product more

and `base` was always just a constant sitting in BASE_STATS. A skill supplying that
base instead of a hardcoded 60 is a small, well-isolated change. Nothing about the
layers, the affix pool, or the currency has to move.

- New item type: weapon, in its own equip slot
- A weapon carries a SKILL, and the skill supplies the bases the layers build on:
  base damage, base speed, base crit chance, base area
- Both playstyles CAN crit. Four of the nine suffixes are crit affixes, so taking
  crit away from casters would kill half their suffix pool. Differentiate by base
  value instead: physical skills get high base crit, spells get near-zero. That is
  the "each skill has base crit chance" note, and it costs nothing
- No resources in this step - resources are 6b

THE AXIS OF DIFFERENCE IS AREA VERSUS SINGLE-TARGET, and the combat layer already
has it. Every stage is trash-then-boss: the trash phase multiplies damage by
min(area, enemyCount), and the boss phase ignores area entirely.

	physical   high per-hit, area 1-2, high base crit   -> melts bosses, slogs trash
	magical    lower per-hit, area 4-8, low base crit   -> shreds trash, grinds bosses

STAGE_TIME_LIMIT_SECONDS is 75 for the TOTAL, so each type has a real failure mode -
pure physical times out on trash, pure magic times out on the boss. That is what
makes the choice a choice rather than a dominance ordering, and it needs no new
systems.

SKILL LEVEL comes from the weapon's item level: floor(itemLevel / 10), plus
modifiers granting "+N to Physical Skill Levels" or "+N to Magical Skill Levels",
weapon-type-locked. Each level raises the skill's BASE by about 4%, so a stage-300
weapon is roughly 2.2x the base of a shallow one.

- This fixes a real gap: item level currently does nothing above stage 80, because
  that is the last tier gate. Skill level makes depth matter forever and makes the
  weapon the chase item
- Side effect worth knowing: skill level raises `base`, so it COMPETES with flat
  damage while increased and more multiply the result. A third position in the layer
  tension, for free
- Risk: this is a second exponential engine beside the gold tracks, which are tuned
  so the feedback exponent stays below 1. At 4% per level it is a modest term rather
  than a second engine, which is why that number and not a bigger one. Making
  weapons BECOME the power curve is the bolder option and would mean retuning the
  gold track exponents down to compensate


## [todo] Phase 6b - The magical playstyle
> Split from 6a because mana is the only genuinely new machinery here, and it is the
> one piece that touches the combat layer rather than the stat layer.

Physical pays STAMINA, magic pays MANA. Both types pay something, so neither is
free - which was the flaw in the first cut of this plan: mana with no matching cost
on physical made magic strictly worse and nobody would ever pick a wand.

ONE resource system, named by the weapon's kind. Same stats, same math, two labels
in the UI. That halves the stat surface and there is no gameplay in having two
implementations of one idea.

	sustained rate = min(speed, regen / costPerUse)
	DPS            = perHit x sustainedRate x critFactor x aoeTargets

Both closed-form, which is non-negotiable: resolveStage has no per-tick loop, and
that is what lets the server resolve offline progress and the harness sweep 300
stages in milliseconds. A tick loop would be correct in the small and would throw
away the two-layer architecture, offline resolution and the balance harness.

THE TWO RESOURCES DIFFER IN WHEN THEY BITE, not in their math:

	              pool    regen   cost    binds
	stamina       small   fast    cheap   LATE, once attack speed is stacked
	mana          large   slow    high    IMMEDIATELY, from the first cast

So physical ignores stamina for a long time and then hits a wall - it has bought
attack speed and is not actually swinging faster, and stamina regen becomes the
affix that unlocks the attack speed it already owns. Magic gears mana from the first
hour or it does not function. Different gearing arcs out of three numbers.

Bonus: stamina is the soft cap attack speed does not currently have. Attack speed is
an uncapped `more` track plus affixes with nothing bounding it.

New affixes, weapon-type-locked:
- resource regen, resource cost reduction (stamina on physical, mana on magical)
- + to Physical Skill Levels, + to Magical Skill Levels

NOT adding a separate cast-speed stat. One speed stat scaled by the skill's own base
speed does the same job; a twin would mean a duplicate of every speed affix for no
difference in outcome.

Weapon-restricted affixes need a tag on AffixDefinition naming which base types may
roll them, plus a filter in rollAffixes. Small schema addition, no migration.

PHYSICAL-VS-MAGICAL PARITY becomes a third invariant beside offence/defence symmetry
and the feedback exponent. Whichever playstyle runs ahead makes the other dead
content, and it will not hold by accident - the offence/defence one has broken
STRUCTURALLY three times now. The test compares TOTAL clear time, not per-phase:
magic is meant to be faster on trash and slower on the boss, and a per-phase check
would fail on the difference that is the whole point.

Two things to measure, not assume:
- Magic may out-earn physical. farmRate is killsPerSecond x goldPerKill and area
  raises kills/sec directly, so unless the lower per-hit offsets it exactly, casters
  become the economic pick as well as the wave pick
- Area is capped at min(area, enemyCount) and enemyCount caps at 220. Past that,
  magic's area stops paying and its scaling has to come from per-hit and speed - so
  magic's late game plays differently from its early game


## [todo] Phase 2 - Unique items
> Moved back behind weapons. Two of the seven uniques below grant or remove EQUIP
> SLOTS, and 6a adds a slot - resolving that interaction once is cheaper than
> resolving it twice. Authoring uniques that can be physical or magical is also only
> possible after skills exist.
>
> If something shipping sooner matters more than authoring once, this can be pulled
> back in front of 6a. The cost is re-tuning eight roll tables and redesigning the
> two slot uniques.

- Unique TIER first, setting drop weight - how hard each one is to obtain. Build the
  mechanism now, fill it in as more uniques are added
- Uniques roll their values within an authored range; Angel Flame is the only
  currency that touches them
- One rule: a unique may carry a downside, and then a fantasy payoff on top
- The seven from the original list:
	- area + (0.25 - 2) / grants 1-3 extra equip slots
	- attack + (2% - 10%) / no crit chance at all, but attack x (150% - 300%)
	- attack + (2% - 10%) / area is 0, but attack speed x (150% - 300%)
	- crit rate - (30% - 50%) / crit damage x (3 - 5)
	- gold drop - (30% - 60%) / stage boss key drops doubled
	- gold drop - (30% - 60%) / dungeon boss currency doubled
	- equip slots - (1-2) / other equipped items' effects x (200% - 500%)
- Add a defensive unique too. Every one above is offence or economy, and a unique
  tier with no defensive chase item fails the symmetry invariant structurally - the
  same fault that hit Phase 1's affix pool and again in 5a-2

Uniques are already the only content using the `more` layer, which is what makes
one feel like a different class of item rather than a rare with a name.


## [todo] Phase 5a-3 - Elements, resistances, penetration
> Deferred twice now, and it belongs AFTER the magical playstyle rather than before.
> Elements are a stat-surface expansion, so they should expand the final damage model
> once - not get built against the physical-only one and then extended to spells.

- Damage splits into pools: physical plus fire, cold, lightning, darkness
- Each pool carries its own flat and increased layers; the gold damage track
  multiplies all of them, or an elemental build has no exponential engine and walls
- Defensive layer gets resistance
- Penetration, subtracted from a target's resistance before it applies

Open design question to settle before starting: four elements against a single
constant enemy resistance would be four NAMES for one stat - identical math, four
times the balance surface, and no decision anywhere. So enemy resistance has to
vary by stage, which means damage swings as you climb and the clear-time band has to
survive it. That is the thing to measure first.

Also unresolved: one penetration stat and one resistance stat, or four of each. Four
of each is a gearing tax that makes sense across a dozen equipment slots; across
four it is book-keeping.

## [todo] Phase 4 - Tablets & the Abyssal dungeon
- Monster waves drop a new currency
	- Tablet, around 0.1% - rare but not hard to obtain given the kill rate
- Tablets are the entry to the Abyssal dungeon
- Clear (n) waves, then the boss
- Tablets have tiers like items (common, magic, rare)
- Tablets carry implicit and explicit modifiers
- Implicit enhances loot, drop rate, gold found
- Explicit is prefix/suffix like an item, but buffs the monsters and the boss
- More explicits is harder and also enhances the implicit. High risk, high return
- Higher tablet tier needs more waves cleared
- Limited HP inside; at zero you leave with nothing
- Tablets get their own tab in the character modal, holding 200 like items
- Most crafting currency works on a tablet

> Authors a whole second affix pool, and its explicit modifiers buff monsters - a
> dimension that does not exist yet. Comes after the formula is settled, and after
> 5a-3 if tablet modifiers are to use elements.
